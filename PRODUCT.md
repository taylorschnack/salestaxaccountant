# Product

## Users

Controllers, CFOs, and finance leads at growth-stage, multistate companies: e-commerce and marketplace sellers, SaaS and software companies, manufacturers and wholesalers, multi-location retailers, often VC-backed. They already know the vocabulary (nexus, taxability, VDA, exemption certificates) and usually arrive with a concrete trigger: crossing an economic nexus threshold, a state notice or audit letter, a diligence or fundraising request, or a messy tax engine setup. They are evaluating whether this specialist can be trusted with the problem, and how fast.

## Product Purpose

The marketing site for a solo sales and use tax specialist (15 years of sales tax experience). Its one job is to turn qualified finance visitors into consult bookings through the contact form. The blog and the nexus checklist exist to build credibility and catch visitors who aren't ready yet; they are secondary to the consult.

Success: a controller lands from search or a referral, sees within seconds that this person handles their exact problem, trusts the expertise, and books a consult.

## Brand Personality

Approachable, modern, fast. The easy, responsive alternative to a Big 4 SALT team: senior expertise without the bureaucracy, explained in plain language. Confident and specific rather than salesy. Speaks to finance people as peers.

The practice is one senior specialist. Copy and design should make the direct, senior attention a selling point rather than hiding it behind a generic "we".

## Anti-references

- **Big 4 corporate**: stock photos of handshakes, dense mega-menus, slow and impersonal.
- **SaaS startup template**: gradient blobs, glass cards, hero-metric rows, purple-to-teal gradients. A previous redesign removed this; don't drift back.
- **Tax software vendor**: Avalara/TaxJar-style product marketing, feature grids, product screenshots. This is advisory, not an engine.
- **Solo-CPA brochure**: dated layouts, clip-art icons, anything that reads as a side practice.

## Design Principles

1. **Specific beats generic.** Name the triggers, states, and deliverables a controller actually deals with. Concrete detail is the trust signal.
2. **Senior and direct.** Show that the client works with the expert, not a junior team. Directness is the value proposition.
3. **Fast to the consult.** Every section should shorten the path to booking; nothing between a ready visitor and the form.
4. **Practice what you preach.** Clear, organized, audit-ready presentation; the site itself should feel like well-kept documentation.
5. **Modern without trend-chasing.** Current and responsive, never templated.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

No formal WCAG target. Best-effort: readable contrast, keyboard-usable forms and navigation, reduced-motion respected. Flag obvious issues.
